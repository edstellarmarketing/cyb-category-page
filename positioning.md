# Edstellar Category Page: Positioning & SEO Framework

**Primary example:** Cybersecurity. All rules apply to every future category page.
**Last updated:** 2026-05-07

## How to read this document

Every rule below is paired with a **Why** block that cites the specific Google guideline it satisfies. Rules without a Why are opinions. Rules with a Why are policy. Follow the ones with a Why without exception; challenge the ones without.

## PART 1: The Google Framework Every Rule Is Built On

Before any keyword or copy decision, understand the two tests Google applies to every page.

### Test 1 — People-First vs Search-Engine-First

Google's systems are designed to surface content created *for people*, not for algorithms. The document asks: *"Is the content primarily made to attract visits from search engines?"* If the answer is yes, the page is search-engine-first content and will be downranked.

**The five questions Google uses to confirm people-first intent:**
1. Do you have an existing or intended audience who would find this useful if they came directly to you?
2. Does the content demonstrate first-hand expertise and depth of knowledge?
3. Does the site have a primary purpose or focus?
4. After reading, will someone feel they've learned enough to achieve their goal?
5. Will someone leave feeling they've had a satisfying experience?

Every content decision on an Edstellar category page must pass all five.

### Test 2 — E-E-A-T (Trust is the most important dimension)

Google's systems evaluate four dimensions: **Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness.** Of these, *"trust is most important."*

For Edstellar category pages, trust is built through:
- Accurate, verifiable claims (program counts, trainer stats, country coverage)
- Specific, named methodology (not vague "expert-led" language)
- Consistent brand voice that matches what enterprise buyers expect

Any copy that reads as inflated, repetitive, or manufactured damages trust and therefore E-E-A-T.

### The site-level risk

Google's helpful content assessment operates **at the site level**, not just the page level. This means a pattern of thin, formulaic category pages can suppress the entire domain — including pages that have strong individual content. This is the primary reason keyword frequency targets were removed from this document.

## PART 2: Universal Rules for Every Category Page

---

### Rule 1 — Each commercial-intent keyword is placed once, in one pinned location

**Rule:** Keywords like `[category] training company`, `[category] corporate training company`, and `[category] training provider` appear **once per page**, in a specific assigned location. They are not targets to hit multiple times.

**Why (Google guideline):**
Google explicitly flags *"content primarily made to attract visits from search engines"* as search-engine-first content. Placing "cybersecurity corporate training company" 8–10 times on one page is a signal that the copy was written for the algorithm, not the reader. It also fails the people-first test: *"Will someone leave feeling they've had a satisfying experience?"* — repeated positioning phrases erode that experience.

A single well-placed, natural use in the right structural position (H1, opening paragraph, or a section heading) is sufficient for Google to understand what Edstellar is. Density beyond that serves rankings, not readers.

**Pinned locations:**

| Keyword | Pinned to | Max uses |
| :--- | :--- | :---: |
| `Corporate Training Company` | Hero Slide 1 — title line 1 | 1 |
| `[category] training company` | WelcomeStrip — Para 1 opening sentence | 1 |
| `[category] corporate training company` | CustomersPartners — goal statement, opening sentence | 1 |
| `[category] training provider` | CustomersPartners — goal statement, closing sentence | 1 |
| `Corporate [category] Training` | Hero eyebrow label + strip sub | 2 |
| `[category] training for employees` | FAQ answer (vendor-selection Q) + one body mention | 2 |

---

### Rule 2 — Meta title uses the corporate positioning keyword; meta description is written for the buyer, not the bot

**Rule:**
- **Meta title format:** `[Category] Corporate Training Company | Edstellar` — max 56 characters
- **Meta description format:** `Edstellar is a global [category] corporate training provider. We deliver instructor-led group training for employees on [Skill 1], [Skill 2], and [Skill 3].` — max 156 characters

**Why (Google guideline):**
Google asks: *"Does the main heading or page title provide a descriptive, helpful summary of the content?"* The meta title is the first impression. `Cybersecurity Corporate Training Company | Edstellar` accurately describes what the page is and who it is for. It is not exaggerated (Google: *"avoid exaggerating or being shocking"*) and it matches search intent for B2B vendor-selection queries.

The description must be written for the buyer reading it in search results, not for keyword density. A buyer scanning results needs to know in one sentence what Edstellar does and for whom.

---

### Rule 3 — Every category page must have substantively unique content

**Rule:** No two category pages may share the same paragraph, FAQ answer, stat card, or section heading with only `[Category]` substituted. Every section must contain content that is specific to that category's skills, compliance landscape, industry verticals, and buyer concerns.

**Why (Google guideline):**
Google directly flags: *"Is the content mass-produced by or outsourced to a large number of creators, or spread across a large network of sites, so that individual pages or sites don't get as much attention or care?"* and *"Are you producing lots of content on many different topics in hopes that some of it might perform well in search results?"*

A template where every field is identical except the category name is the definition of thin, mass-produced content. Google's helpful content system evaluates site-wide patterns. A pattern of 20 category pages with identical structure and swapped nouns will suppress the entire domain.

**What "unique" means in practice:**
- Hero Slide 1 description names certifications and domains specific to that category (not generic)
- WelcomeStrip Para 1 names real programs (CISSP, CEH, PMP, SHRM — whatever applies)
- Stats section uses data points sourced from the category's industry research
- FAQ answers address objections that buyers in that category actually raise
- CustomersPartners pillars use stat figures pulled from that category's real program count

---

### Rule 4 — Claims must be verifiable and accurate

**Rule:** Every statistic, program count, country count, and trainer count cited on the page must be pulled from a confirmed source (edstellar.com program listings, published reports, or internal data). No invented or approximated figures.

**Why (Google guideline):**
Google's expertise self-assessment asks: *"Does the content have any easily-verified factual errors?"* and *"Does the content present information in a way that makes you want to trust it, such as clear sourcing, evidence of expertise?"*

Fabricated stats ("200+ programs" on a category that has 40) destroy E-E-A-T. If a CISO fact-checks the page and finds inflated numbers, trust is lost — and lost trust is a behavioral signal (bounces, no return visits) that feeds directly into ranking systems.

---

### Rule 5 — Do not produce content about a category Edstellar cannot genuinely serve

**Rule:** A category page should only be built for a training domain where Edstellar has vetted trainers, real programs, and delivery history. Do not create a page for a category because it has search volume.

**Why (Google guideline):**
Google warns: *"Did you decide to enter some niche topic area without any real expertise, but instead mainly because you thought you'd get search traffic?"* This is a direct signal of search-engine-first intent. A category page built purely for SEO capture, for a domain Edstellar cannot actually deliver in, will eventually be exposed by bounce rates, zero enquiries, and the inability to convert — all negative ranking signals.

---

### Rule 6 — "Courses" is eliminated from all headings, CTAs, and primary navigation

**Rule:** Use "program", "training program", or "training programs" everywhere. "Courses" is permitted only inside FAQ answers where it appears in a buyer's question phrasing.

**Why (Google guideline):**
This is a brand voice rule, not a Google policy rule. However, it connects to the E-E-A-T Trust dimension: the language Edstellar uses must match how enterprise L&D buyers speak. "Courses" signals a retail, consumer context. Enterprise L&D Directors, CISOs, and procurement teams evaluate "programs" and "workforce development initiatives" — not "courses". Copy that uses retail language signals to a B2B buyer that the vendor doesn't understand their world, damaging trust.

---

### Rule 7 — No accreditation claims, no certification body affiliation claims

**Rule:** Never write:
- "Edstellar-certified"
- "Certified by Edstellar"
- "Official training partner of [body]"
- "Authorized training provider for [body]" (e.g., EC-Council, ISC2, PMI, SHRM)
- Anything that implies Edstellar grants or is affiliated with industry certifications

**Why (Google guideline):**
Google asks: *"Does the content have any easily-verified factual errors?"* Claiming an affiliation that does not exist is a factual error. Enterprise buyers verify vendor credentials before purchase. A claim that Edstellar is an "authorized EC-Council training partner" when it is not will be discovered and will destroy trust at the highest-value stage of the buying journey. It also creates legal exposure.

Edstellar's trainers hold certifications; Edstellar itself does not grant them. This distinction must be preserved in all copy.

---

### Rule 8 — No em-dashes, no exaggerated superlatives, no vague trust language

**Rule:**
- Replace every em-dash (—) with a period, comma, or colon
- Do not use "world-class", "best-in-class", "cutting-edge", "unparalleled", or similar superlatives without specific evidence
- Do not use "expert trainers" as a standalone claim — always follow it with the vetting mechanism that makes it true

**Why (Google guideline):**
Google's content quality questions ask: *"Does the main heading or page title avoid exaggerating or being shocking in nature?"* and *"Is the content produced well, or does it appear sloppy or hastily produced?"* Superlatives without evidence are a form of exaggeration. Em-dashes are a stylistic rule from Edstellar's brand guidelines. Vague trust language ("expert trainers") reads as a claim without substance — the specific vetting process (multi-stage assessment, trial session, client feedback) is what earns the claim, and that specificity is what builds E-E-A-T.

### Hero Section (HeroSlider.tsx)

**Slide 1 — the corporate identity slide**

| Element | Rule | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Eyebrow label** | "Corporate [Category] Training" | Establishes the corporate (not consumer) context before the headline. One of two permitted uses of this phrase. |
| **Title line 1** | "Corporate Training Company" | Positions Edstellar's identity in the largest typographic element on the page. Satisfies Google's *"descriptive, helpful summary"* requirement for the heading. |
| **Title line 2** | "Built for Enterprise [Outcome]." — outcome must be specific to the category (e.g., "Workforce Upskilling", "Risk Reduction", "Leadership Depth") | Completes the positioning with a buyer-relevant outcome signal. Avoids vague language. |
| **Description** | Must contain: `instructor-led`, `upskill and reskill`, `group training`, `[N]+ programs`, `measurable outcomes`. No em-dashes. | Each term targets a distinct B2B buyer concern. | **Primary CTA** | "Browse Training Programs" → links to #catalog | "Programs" not "Courses". Intra-page anchor only. |
| **Secondary CTA** | "Enquire Now" → links to #contact | Retained as-is. Change to "Book a Capability Discovery Call" is a UX decision pending separate approval. |

**Slides 2–4 — industry vertical slides**

Each must name a specific industry and its compliance or regulatory context. Generic industry names without named regulation (e.g., "Healthcare" without HIPAA/HITRUST) are not sufficient. This is the uniqueness signal that tells Google these slides serve real buyers, not search crawlers.

### FAQ Section (CyberFAQ.tsx / per-category FAQ)

| Rule | Why |
| :--- | :--- |
| 8–12 questions per page, no fewer, no more | Google's people-first test: *"After reading, will someone leave feeling they've learned enough to achieve their goal?"* 8–12 covers the full pre-purchase decision loop without becoming padding. Fewer leaves buyer objections unanswered; more signals content inflation. |
| Every answer is one paragraph, no bullets or sub-headings inside the answer | FAQ answers are conversational content. Bullet points inside an accordion answer signal that the writer was thinking about on-page SEO structure, not the reader's question. Google can detect this. |
| Answer length: 200–320 characters. Never exceed 320. | Within this range the answer is complete without padding. Answers longer than 320 are padded — they contain filler to hit a perceived word-count target, which Google explicitly warns against: *"Are you writing to a particular word count because you've heard that Google has a preferred word count? No, we don't."* |
| `instructor-led` must appear in at least 3 answers | Edstellar's primary USP must be present in the section buyers use to make final vendor decisions. |
| `group training` or `cohort` must appear in at least 2 answers | Differentiates from individual/self-paced. |
| At least one answer must define ILT and VILT clearly | Many enterprise buyers are unfamiliar with the acronyms. Defining them serves the reader (people-first) and signals expertise (E-E-A-T). |
| Never mention specific pricing figures | Specific prices date the page. Changed prices with unchanged page dates are a signal Google flags: *"Are you changing the date of pages to make them seem fresh when the content has not substantially changed?"* Direct to #contact instead. |
| **Mandatory Q: vendor selection** | Add: *"How do we select the right [category] corporate training provider?"* Answer positions Edstellar by describing the criteria a smart buyer should use — instructor-led delivery, lab customization, global scale, managed services model. | This is the highest-intent FAQ a B2B buyer searching `"[category] corporate training provider"` will want answered. Answering it thoroughly is people-first; it also naturally places the "training provider" keyword once in a completely organic context, satisfying Google's test: *"Is the content primarily made to attract visits from search engines?"* — No: this question serves a real buyer need. |
| **Mandatory Q: group vs individual** | Add: *"Why is group training more effective for employees than individual licenses?"* Answer covers team alignment, shared threat models, collective capability building, measurable team-level outcomes. | Answers the objection raised by buyers comparing Edstellar (group, instructor-led) to Coursera/Udemy (individual, self-paced). This is a real buyer question — people-first. It also naturally includes `group training for employees` in a substantive context. |

## PART 4: Keyword Placement Map (Not a Frequency Target)

The table below is a **placement map**, not a count target. Each keyword has one home. Using it in that home once is correct. Using it three times in that home, or moving it to additional sections, is keyword stuffing.

| Keyword | Home location | Max uses on page |
| :--- | :--- | :---: |
| `Corporate Training Company` | Hero Slide 1 — title line 1 | 1 |
| `[category] training company` | WelcomeStrip — Para 1, opening sentence | 1 |
| `[category] corporate training company` | CustomersPartners — goal statement, sentence 1 | 1 |
| `[category] training provider` | CustomersPartners — goal statement, final sentence | 1 |
| `Corporate [category] Training` | Hero eyebrow + strip sub label | 2 |
| `[category] training for employees` | FAQ vendor-selection answer + one body mention | 2 |
| `instructor-led` | All sections — no cap, this is Edstellar's core USP | 10+ |
| `group training` | FAQ, Pricing, About, Hero description | 8+ |
| `customized instructor-led training` | WelcomeStrip Para 2 (bolded), About, Pricing, Contact | 4 |
| `upskill / upskilling` | Hero, Welcome, About, Pricing, FAQ | 5+ |
| `reskill / reskilling` | Hero, Welcome, About, Blog, FAQ | 4+ |
| `[category] Training Programs` (generic) | Reduce in headings. Prefer "Corporate [category] Training" or "Enterprise-grade programs" in H2s | < 5 in headings |
| `[category] Training Courses` (visible text) | Eliminated from headings, CTAs, nav | 0 |

**Why this structure instead of frequency targets (Google guideline):**
Google warns: *"Are you producing lots of content on many different topics in hopes that some of it might perform well in search results?"* Frequency targets produce pages optimised for counts, not for readers. A placement map produces pages where each keyword appears once in the location where it is most useful to the buyer — which is the definition of people-first content.

## PART 5: What to Never Do (and Why)

| Prohibited | Why (Google guideline) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Copy a paragraph from one category page to another with `[Category]` substituted | *"Mass-produced content where individual pages don't get as much attention or care"* — direct helpful content flag |
| Set a keyword frequency target (e.g., "use this phrase 8+ times") | Frequency targets produce algorithm-first content. Google: *"Is the content primarily made to attract visits from search engines?"* |
| Change page dates without substantially updating content | Google explicitly flags: *"Are you changing the date of pages to make them seem fresh when the content has not substantially changed?"* |
| Write about a training category Edstellar cannot genuinely deliver | *"Did you decide to enter some niche topic area without any real expertise, but instead mainly because you thought you'd get search traffic?"* |
| Claim accreditation or certification body affiliation | *"Does the content have any easily-verified factual errors?"* — such a claim is one. |
| Use invented or unverifiable statistics | Same as above. Every stat must trace to a real source. |
| Use "courses" in headings or CTAs | Brand/tone rule. Damages E-E-A-T Trust dimension with enterprise buyers. |
| Use em-dashes | Edstellar brand rule. |
| Write FAQ answers longer than 320 characters | Padding. Google: *"Are you writing to a particular word count?"* — padding is the same instinct. |
| Add new content or remove old content primarily to appear "fresh" | Google directly addresses this: *"Are you adding lots of new content or removing older content primarily because you believe it will help your search rankings? No, it won't."* |
